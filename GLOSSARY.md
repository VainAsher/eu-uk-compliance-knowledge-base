# Glossary

Shared, authoritative definitions of terms of art used across the knowledge base. Documents should reference this glossary for common terms and reserve their own Definitions section for document-specific or narrowed usages.

**Convention:** Each term cites its authoritative source. Where a term is defined in legislation, the statutory definition governs. Keys in the *Source* column reference `SOURCE_REGISTRY.md`.

**Last updated:** 2026-07-11 (sub-topics batch 2: added DPIA, prior consultation, GPAI systemic risk, GPAI Code of Practice, CMP, TC String, consent or pay, cookie wall, TIBER-EU, data protection test, Digital Omnibus)

| Term | Definition | Source |
|------|------------|--------|
| Accountability | The controller's obligation to be responsible for, and able to *demonstrate* compliance with, the data-protection principles (GDPR Art. 5(2)). | eurlex-gdpr |
| Anonymous data | Data that does not relate to an identified or identifiable natural person, or is rendered anonymous such that the subject is no longer identifiable; falls outside the GDPR (Recital 26). Contrast *pseudonymisation*. | eurlex-gdpr |
| Consent | Any freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous indication of the data subject's wishes by a statement or clear affirmative action (GDPR Art. 4(11)). | eurlex-gdpr |
| Controller | The natural or legal person, public authority, agency or other body which, alone or jointly, determines the purposes and means of processing (GDPR Art. 4(7)). | eurlex-gdpr |
| Data subject | An identified or identifiable natural person to whom personal data relates (GDPR Art. 4(1)). | eurlex-gdpr |
| Personal data | Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person; identifiability may be direct or indirect (GDPR Art. 4(1)). Construed broadly (Nowak; Breyer). | eurlex-gdpr / case-nowak / case-breyer |
| Processing | Any operation performed on personal data, whether or not by automated means (collection, storage, use, disclosure, erasure, etc.) (GDPR Art. 4(2)). | eurlex-gdpr |
| Processor | A natural or legal person, public authority, agency or other body that processes personal data on behalf of the controller (GDPR Art. 4(8)). | eurlex-gdpr |
| Pseudonymisation | Processing personal data so it can no longer be attributed to a specific data subject without additional information kept separately and secured (GDPR Art. 4(5)). Pseudonymised data remains *personal data*. | eurlex-gdpr |
| Special category data | Personal data revealing racial/ethnic origin, political opinions, religious/philosophical beliefs, trade-union membership; genetic, biometric, health, sex-life or sexual-orientation data (GDPR Art. 9). | eurlex-gdpr |
| Supervisory authority | An independent public authority established by a Member State to monitor the application of the GDPR (GDPR Art. 4(21), Art. 51). In the UK, the ICO / Information Commission. | eurlex-gdpr |
| UK GDPR | Regulation (EU) 2016/679 as retained/assimilated in UK domestic law under EUWA 2018 s.3 and amended for the UK context; read together with the DPA 2018. | euwa-2018-s3 / legislation-uk-dpa2018 |
| Assimilated law | Status (from 1 January 2024) of former "retained EU law", incl. the UK GDPR, following the Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023. | euwa-2018-s3 |
| Adequacy decision | A European Commission finding that a third country ensures an adequate level of protection, permitting EEA→that-country transfers without further safeguards. The UK holds adequacy renewed to 27 December 2031. | ec-adequacy-decisions / ec-uk-adequacy-2025 |
| Lex specialis | A specific law that prevails over a general law on the matters it specifically governs (e.g. the ePrivacy Directive over the GDPR for electronic communications). | eurlex-eprivacy-consolidated |
| Cookie rule (Art. 5(3)) | The ePrivacy requirement to obtain consent before storing/accessing information on a user's terminal equipment, unless strictly necessary for a service the user requested. | eurlex-eprivacy-consolidated |
| PECR | Privacy and Electronic Communications (EC Directive) Regulations 2003 — the UK transposition of the ePrivacy Directive. | uk-pecr-2003 |
| Data protection by design & by default (DPbDD) | The GDPR Art. 25 obligation to embed data-protection principles into processing from the design stage, and to process only necessary data by default. | eurlex-gdpr / edpb-guidelines-4-2019 |
| Technical and organisational measures (TOMs) | The combined technical and organisational controls used to meet GDPR Arts. 24, 25 and 32. | eurlex-gdpr |
| Privacy-enhancing technology (PET) | A technology that minimises personal-data processing or protects it (e.g. pseudonymisation, encryption, differential privacy, aggregation). | enisa-pbd |
| Differential privacy | A formal mathematical guarantee that including or excluding any single individual's data does not significantly change an analysis's output. | enisa-pbd / hoepman-pds |
| AI system | A machine-based system operating with varying autonomy that infers from inputs how to generate outputs (predictions, content, recommendations, decisions) influencing environments (AI Act Art. 3(1)). | eurlex-aiact-eli |
| GPAI model | A general-purpose AI model with significant generality, capable of a wide range of distinct tasks (AI Act Art. 3(63)). | eurlex-aiact-eli |
| High-risk AI system | An AI system within AI Act Art. 6 and Annex I/III, subject to the Chapter III obligations. | eurlex-aiact-eli / ec-ai-framework |
| AI Office | The European Commission body overseeing GPAI and supporting AI Act implementation and enforcement. | ec-ai-framework |
| AI governance | The organisational structures, policies, roles and processes that direct, resource and assure responsible development, deployment and use of AI systems. | iso-42001 / oecd-ai-principles |
| AI management system (AIMS) | Interrelated elements an organisation uses to set AI policies/objectives and processes for responsible AI development and use (ISO/IEC 42001). | iso-42001 |
| Trustworthy AI | AI that is valid/reliable, safe, secure/resilient, accountable/transparent, explainable, privacy-enhanced and fair with harmful bias managed (NIST characterisation). | nist-ai-rmf |
| Human oversight | Measures enabling natural persons to understand, monitor, intervene in or override an AI system's operation (AI Act Art. 14). | eurlex-aiact-eli |
| Connected product | An item that obtains, generates or collects data about its use/environment and can communicate product data (Data Act Art. 2(5)). | eurlex-dataact |
| Data holder | A person with the right or obligation to use and make available data, including product/related-service data (Data Act Art. 2(13)). | eurlex-dataact |
| Data processing service | An on-demand, scalable cloud or edge computing service (Data Act Art. 2(8)). | eurlex-dataact |
| Intermediary service | A "mere conduit", "caching" or "hosting" service (DSA Art. 3(g)). | eurlex-dsa |
| Online platform | A hosting service that stores and disseminates information to the public at a recipient's request (DSA Art. 3(i)). | eurlex-dsa |
| VLOP / VLOSE | Very Large Online Platform / Search Engine designated for ≥45m average monthly active EU recipients (DSA Art. 33). | eurlex-dsa |
| Digital Services Coordinator (DSC) | The independent national authority supervising DSA compliance and coordinating at EU level (DSA Art. 49). | eurlex-dsa |
| Gatekeeper | An undertaking providing a core platform service, designated by the Commission under DMA Art. 3 (significant impact + important gateway + entrenched/durable position). | eurlex-dma |
| Core platform service (CPS) | One of the DMA Art. 2(2) service categories to which gatekeeper obligations attach. | eurlex-dma |
| Essential entity | Entity classified "essential" under NIS2 Art. 3(1) — broadly large entities in Annex I high-criticality sectors plus specified types regardless of size; ex-ante + ex-post supervision. | eurlex-nis2 |
| Important entity | An in-scope NIS2 entity that is not an essential entity (Art. 3(2)); ex-post supervision only. | eurlex-nis2 |
| Significant incident | NIS2 incident causing or capable of causing severe operational disruption/financial loss or considerable damage to others (Art. 23(3)). | eurlex-nis2 |
| CSIRT | Computer Security Incident Response Team designated by each Member State (NIS2 Art. 10), receiving incident reports and providing response support. | eurlex-nis2 |
| Digital operational resilience | A financial entity's ability to build, assure and review its operational integrity/reliability so it can keep providing services through ICT disruptions (DORA Art. 3(1)). | eurlex-dora |
| Critical ICT third-party provider (CTPP) | An ICT third-party provider designated critical by the ESAs (DORA Art. 31), brought within the Union Oversight Framework. | eurlex-dora |
| Threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) | Controlled test of live production systems mimicking real threat actors' TTPs, based on targeted threat intelligence (DORA Arts. 3(17), 26). | eurlex-dora |
| CIA triad | Confidentiality, integrity and availability — the three core security objectives; GDPR Art. 32 also names resilience. | eurlex-gdpr |
| Information Security Management System (ISMS) | A systematic, risk-based framework of policies, processes and controls for protecting information, specified by ISO/IEC 27001. | iso-27001 |
| Zero trust | A security model removing implicit network-location trust; continuous per-resource verification (NIST SP 800-207). | nist-sp-800-207 |
| Product with digital elements | A software/hardware product with a data connection, regulated for cybersecurity by the Cyber Resilience Act. | eurlex-cra |
| Consumer | A natural person acting wholly or mainly outside their trade, business, craft or profession (CRD Art. 2(1); CRA 2015 s.2(3)). | eurlex-crd / uk-cra-2015 |
| Trader | A person acting for purposes relating to their trade, business, craft or profession in consumer transactions (CRD Art. 2(2)). | eurlex-crd |
| Right of withdrawal | A consumer's right to withdraw from a distance/off-premises contract within 14 days without reason or cost (CRD Arts. 9–16). | eurlex-crd |
| Unfair commercial practice | A B2C practice contrary to professional diligence that materially distorts consumers' economic behaviour, or is misleading/aggressive, or blacklisted (UCPD; UK DMCCA). | eurlex-ucpd / uk-dmcca-2024 |
| Dark pattern | Interface/choice-architecture design that materially distorts a user's ability to make free, informed choices (e.g. drip pricing, obstructed cancellation). | eurlex-dsa / eurlex-ucpd |
| Information-society service (ISS) | A service normally provided for remuneration, at a distance, by electronic means at a recipient's individual request; online games are ISS. | eurlex-gdpr / dpa-2018-s9 |
| Children's Code (AADC) | ICO statutory code (DPA 2018 s.123) setting 15 design standards for online services likely to be accessed by children. | dpa-2018-s123 / ico-childrens-code |
| Loot box | An optional in-game purchase where the player does not know what they receive before buying (loot boxes, card packs, gacha). | pegi-labels / gov-loot-boxes-update |
| Shared-responsibility model | Division of security/compliance controls between cloud provider (security "of" the cloud) and customer (security "in" the cloud); the split shifts across IaaS/PaaS/SaaS. | eurlex-gdpr / iso-27001 |
| EUCS | Candidate European Cybersecurity Certification Scheme for cloud services under the Cybersecurity Act; not yet adopted. | eurlex-csa2019 / enisa-eucs |
| Compliance management system (CMS) | Interrelated elements an organisation uses to set compliance policy/objectives and processes, embedding a compliance culture (ISO 37301). | iso-37301 |
| Three Lines Model | IIA governance model: first line (operational risk ownership), second line (risk/compliance oversight), third line (independent internal audit); 2020 update of "three lines of defence". | iia-three-lines |
| Records of Processing Activities (RoPA) | The GDPR Art. 30 record of processing operations (purposes, categories, recipients, transfers, retention, security). | eurlex-gdpr |
| Restricted transfer | A transfer of personal data to a third country/international organisation that triggers GDPR Chapter V. | eurlex-gdpr |
| Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) | Commission-approved modular model clauses providing Art. 46(2)(c) transfer safeguards; current set Decision (EU) 2021/914. | eurlex-sccs-2021 |
| Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs) | Supervisory-authority-approved intra-group data-protection rules enabling group transfers (GDPR Art. 47). | eurlex-gdpr |
| Transfer impact assessment (TIA) | The Schrems II case-by-case assessment of whether a destination ensures essentially equivalent protection, and what supplementary measures are needed. | case-schrems2 / edpb-rec-01-2020 |
| International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA) | The ICO's standalone UK Art. 46 transfer contract; alternative to EU SCCs + UK Addendum. | ico-idta-addendum |
| Recognised legitimate interest | A specified public-interest purpose (DUAA-inserted UK GDPR Annex) for which processing needs no legitimate-interests balancing test. | ico-duaa-rli |
| Conformity assessment | The AI Act / CRA process of demonstrating a product/system meets the applicable requirements (via internal control or a notified body). | eurlex-aiact-eli / eurlex-cra |
| Notified body | A conformity-assessment body designated to perform third-party conformity assessment under EU harmonisation law (AI Act; CRA). | eurlex-aiact-eli |
| Fundamental-rights impact assessment (FRIA) | The AI Act Art. 27 assessment certain deployers must run before deploying an Annex III high-risk AI system. | eurlex-aiact-eli |
| Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) | A machine-readable inventory of a product's software components/dependencies; required for CRA vulnerability handling. | eurlex-cra |
| Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) | An assessment of the impact of envisaged processing on data protection, required where processing is likely to result in a high risk to individuals (GDPR Art. 35). | eurlex-gdpr / wp248-dpia |
| Prior consultation | The GDPR Art. 36 duty to consult the supervisory authority before processing where a DPIA shows a high residual risk the controller cannot mitigate. | eurlex-gdpr |
| GPAI systemic risk | Risk specific to the high-impact capabilities of general-purpose AI models with significant Union-level impact; presumed above 10²⁵ FLOP training compute (AI Act Arts. 3(65), 51). | eurlex-aiact-eli |
| GPAI Code of Practice | Voluntary co-regulatory instrument (published 10 July 2025; Transparency, Copyright, Safety & Security chapters) used to demonstrate AI Act Art. 53/55 compliance pending harmonised standards. | gpai-code-of-practice-final / ec-gpai-cop |
| Consent-management platform (CMP) | A tool that presents cookie/consent choices, records the decision and signals it to tags so non-essential technologies fire only with consent. | edpb-guidelines-2-2023 |
| TC String | Encoded string under the IAB TCF recording a user's consent/objection preferences; held to be personal data in IAB Europe (C-604/22). | case-iab-europe |
| Consent or pay ("pay-or-okay") | A binary model requiring the user to consent to tracking-based advertising or pay a fee for a tracking-free version; addressed in EDPB Opinion 08/2024. | edpb-opinion-08-2024 |
| Cookie wall | Blocking access unless the user consents to non-essential cookies, leaving no genuine choice; invalidates "freely given" consent. | edpb-guidelines-05-2020 |
| TIBER-EU | The Eurosystem Threat Intelligence-Based Ethical Red Teaming framework; recognised delivery vehicle for DORA threat-led penetration testing. | ecb-tiber-eu |
| Data protection test | The DUAA 2025 transfers standard — whether protection in the destination is "not materially lower" than under UK law — governing both UK adequacy regulations and exporter TRAs. | duaa-2025-sch7 / ico-tra |
| Digital Omnibus | The EU's late-2025 simplification package: an *adopted* Digital Omnibus on AI (amending the AI Act) and a *proposed* Digital Omnibus Regulation (data/ePrivacy strand). | ep-legtrain-digital-omnibus-ai |

> Terms are added as documents introduce them. Do not redefine a glossary term inconsistently in a document; if a narrower meaning is needed, state it explicitly as document-specific.
